Why the calculation method matters

Different test manuals may specify different conventions. Calendar age counts complete calendar months and remaining days. The 30-day option uses a 30-day borrowed month. Neither should be assumed to suit every assessment. Always follow the manual for the particular assessment and edition.

Important: This tool performs date arithmetic, not diagnostic advice, psychometric scoring, test selection or norm-table decisions.

Frequently asked questions

What is chronological age in psychological assessment?

It is the time between someone's date of birth and a reference date, commonly expressed in years, months and days.

Should I use today's date or the date of assessment?

Use the date required by the assessment manual, commonly the actual testing date.

Why do some assessment age calculations use 30-day months?

Some manuals specify a subtraction procedure with 30 days borrowed from a month. Other manuals use different rules.

Do I round chronological age to the nearest month?

Only if the applicable test manual instructs you to do so.

Can I use this for SSAIS, JSAIS, WISC or other psychological assessments?

The date arithmetic can help, but you must verify the correct age convention, age ranges and norm table using the manual for the specific assessment edition.

What does 8;4;12 mean?

Eight years, four months and twelve days.

Does this calculator store client data?

Can I calculate age for an assessment completed in the past?

Yes. Enter the historical assessment date.

What if the assessment date is before the birth date?

The calculator reports an error.

How are leap years and birthdays on the 29th, 30th or 31st handled?

For the calendar method, anniversaries falling outside shorter months are clamped to the last day of the month. Check the test manual before using that convention.

Is chronological age the same as developmental age?

No. Chronological age is date-based. Developmental age concerns developmental functioning, which date subtraction cannot determine.
